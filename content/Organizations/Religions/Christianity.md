#Religion #Organization

**Type:** World Religion
**Also Known As:** Christendom, The Faith
**Holy Text:** The Bible (Old and New Testaments)
**Founder:** Jesus Christ
**Holy City:** [[Cytropodo]] (seat of the Papacy)

---

## Overview

Christianity is the primary faith within Europe and the dominant civilizational force of the western world. Based on the teachings of Jesus Christ and the revelations given to the Jewish people, the Christian Church claims universal spiritual authority over all baptized souls and, by extension, temporal influence over the kingdoms that profess the faith. Christians are responsible for the majority of technological and cultural progress of the last age — the construction of cathedrals, the founding of universities, the preservation of classical learning, and the development of legal and philosophical traditions that underpin western governance. Though far from perfect, they represent the best that western civilization has to offer.

---

## Jesus Christ

Jesus of Nazareth was born in Bethlehem in 0 BC to the Virgin Mary, conceived by the Holy Spirit. Raised as a carpenter's son in Galilee, He began His public ministry around age 30, preaching repentance, the coming Kingdom of God, and radical teachings on mercy, sacrifice, and the nature of divine love.

He gathered twelve apostles and performed miracles — healing the sick, raising the dead, and commanding nature. His teachings challenged both the Jewish religious establishment and Roman authority. He was arrested, tried before the Sanhedrin and the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, and crucified outside Jerusalem around 33 AD.

He rose from the dead on the third day, appeared to His disciples over forty days, and ascended into heaven — commissioning His followers to spread the Gospel to all nations. This resurrection is the foundation of the entire faith.

## Core Beliefs

**The Trinity:** God exists as three persons in one essence — the Father, the Son (Jesus Christ), and the Holy Spirit. This doctrine distinguishes Christianity from both Judaism and Islam.

**Original Sin and Redemption:** Humanity is fallen through the sin of Adam and Eve. Salvation comes not through human effort alone but through the sacrificial death and resurrection of Christ. Grace is received through faith and the sacraments.

**The Seven Sacraments:**
- **Baptism** — Entry into the Church; cleansing of original sin
- **Confirmation** — Strengthening of the Holy Spirit
- **Eucharist** — The body and blood of Christ, received at Mass
- **Confession** — Absolution of sins through a priest
- **Anointing of the Sick** — Spiritual healing and preparation for death
- **Holy Orders** — Ordination of priests, deacons, and bishops
- **Matrimony** — Sacred union between man and woman

**On Non-Believers:** Christianity holds a universal mandate to evangelize all peoples. Non-believers are viewed through several lenses:
- **Jews** — The original covenant people who rejected Christ. Tolerated but pressured to convert. Their scriptures are honored as the Old Testament, but their refusal of Jesus as Messiah places them outside salvation.
- **Muslims** — Followers of a false prophet. The primary civilizational enemy of Christendom. The crusades were waged to reclaim territory from Muslim expansion and defend Christian populations.
- **Pagans** — Worshippers of demons or false gods. Subject to evangelization and, where politically feasible, suppression of their practices.
- **Heretics** — Baptized Christians who hold false doctrine. Considered worse than non-believers, as they corrupt the faith from within. Subject to investigation and ecclesiastical punishment.

**Just War:** The Church holds that war can be morally licit under specific conditions — legitimate authority, just cause, right intention, and proportional means. The crusades were proclaimed under this doctrine. In practice, the gap between the theology and the conduct of Christian armies has been significant.

## Structure

- **Pope** — The Vicar of Christ and supreme head of the Church, claiming authority traced back to the Apostle Peter. Resides in [[Cytropodo]].
- **Cardinals** — Senior clergy who advise the Pope and elect his successor. Often hold significant political influence in their own right.
- **Archbishops** — Oversee provinces containing multiple dioceses. Hold authority over the bishops beneath them.
- **Bishops** — Govern individual dioceses. Responsible for ordaining priests, administering sacraments, and maintaining doctrine within their territory.
- **Priests** — The primary ministers of the faith. Celebrate Mass, hear confessions, perform baptisms and marriages, and serve as the Church's presence in every parish.
- **Deacons** — Ordained ministers who assist priests and bishops. Often serve the poor and administer charity.
- **Monks and Nuns** — Members of religious orders who take vows of poverty, chastity, and obedience. Monasteries serve as centers of learning, agriculture, and manuscript preservation.

### Military Orders

The Church also maintains or sanctions several military orders that serve as its armed instruments:
- [[Order of the Radiant Cross]] — The Church's primary militant and peacekeeping arm
- [[Knights of the Iron Covenant]] — A secular-military order that became the nobility of conquered territories
- [[Brotherhood of the Penitent Blade]] — A monastic order devoted to holy suffering and sacrifice
- [[Hospitallers of St. Andrian]] — A healing order pressed into military service

## In the World

Christianity is the organizing principle of European civilization. Its calendar structures the year. Its morality underpins the law. Its clergy are the most literate class in most kingdoms, and its monasteries preserve the knowledge of antiquity. Kings derive their legitimacy from coronation by bishops, and even the most powerful secular rulers must negotiate with the Church rather than simply command it.

The [[First Crusade]] (1032–1152) and [[Second Crusade]] were proclaimed by the Church and prosecuted by its military orders, resulting in the Christian occupation of significant Muslim territories across northern Africa. These campaigns demonstrated both the reach of Christian military power and the moral cost of exercising it — the atrocities committed by orders like the [[Order of the Radiant Cross]] remain a source of internal controversy.

The Church's influence is not purely spiritual. It collects tithes, holds vast tracts of land, operates courts with jurisdiction over clergy and matters of marriage and heresy, and maintains diplomatic networks that rival those of any kingdom. Papal decrees can redirect the foreign policy of entire nations.
