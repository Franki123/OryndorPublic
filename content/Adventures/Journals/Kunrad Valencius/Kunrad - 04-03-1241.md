We arrived by boat to Lektas. A city taken by my order in the first crusade. The first thing that came to was the stench. The saracen had a particular smell that I have learned to identify. The city reeked of it. The preachers were doing a poor job, to have an entire city reject his holy truth? The Radiant Cross would have done a far superior job.  
  
Upon getting off the others went in search of alcohol, because that is their way. I went to the church, so I could do my prayers. The cathedral was amazing, a true house of God, proof he was there even in the darkest of places. I looked upon the beauty of the stained glass and remembered something I once heard, of a peasant who, though they could not read nor write, saw the windows on the church and knew the glory of the Lord. Indeed, I gazed upon them and knew God is truly great.  
  
I prayed before the alter, thanksgiving him for his gifts, for allowing me the strength to keep my oaths, for guiding my hand, for always being there, even when all others failed. I asked him to help guide me through this quest, to find the Black Hand and to give me the strength to fight back evil wherever I found it.  
  
  
It was here that the bishop came before me. He knew my Order and knew we could be counted on, so he gave me the task: find the militant leaders of the rebellion. I accepted of course, for it is the duty of all good Christians to fight evil in any way, whenever and wherever it is found.  
  
I went to search for my group to relay the information but as always the pagan and the drunkard were displaced and unhelpful. The Scot was at least willing to listen, as well the singer. In the end I was on my own. I spent the day patrolling the districts, seeking out the areas of deepest Saracen corruption. They are keen to throw rocks and debris in futile efforts to resist the authority of the Almighty's Church, a fact true across the land.  
  
  
As I did this, and as day gave way to evening, there was a fire. Naturally, as any true Christian, I went there at once to render aid. After helping contain the fire, it was reported to me that within was a family of 5. Such barbarity to kill without reason. I knew that Moment the ones behind this were vile and godless.  
  
  
It was little surprise when I discovered from the Singer that it was, in fact, the Pagan. She had casually thrown a fireball into the house, killing everyone. I have seen pagans do much and killing those weaker than themselves is a common, almost universal, trait among their kin. Requiring her aid still, I resolved to only beat her into unconsciousness with my fists instead of killing her. She tried to attack back with her pagan witchery, yet God protected, as he always does, and she failed.
