## The Bridge

I left the bridge before the bloodshed started.

[[Kunrad Valencius|Kunrad]] stayed behind. Of course he did. He’s always so certain that standing in front of a storm and daring it to break is the same thing as righteousness. I told him plainly I would not fight against people who only want to be free. He answered with God. He’s not worried about the chains around their necks. He’s embraced his and called them jewelry. 

So I left. 

[[William Cromwell|William]] came with me, and [[Lucero De La Cruz|Lucero]] as well. That surprised me less. William sees rot where others see order, and Lucero waits until things make sense before choosing a side. Smart. 

We went for [[Baldwin Greystone|Baldwin]], because whatever is happening in this city isn’t the shouting in the streets. That makes sense. People always fight when they’re caged. What Baldwin is building does not.

## The Keep

We found him at the center of it, giving orders while his people cut down civilians. Using his constructs to commit further atrocities. They are wrong. We’ve seen them before, but it never stops hitting me the same way. They’re hollow in a way I can feel before I understand it, like something has been taken out of the world and forced into them. 

Baldwin calls it a power source. The rest of us would call it a soul. That is not metaphor, not faith, not poetry. A soul, bound and used like tinder on a fire. When the dead cry out, I hear them.

William reacted how I expected. Rage, but the kind that thinks it’s holy. He called them abominations. For once, I agree with him, but not for the reason he thinks. This isn’t blasphemy, it’s slavery. It’s theft. Baldwin is taking what should pass on and chaining it to iron. And suddenly the church, the machines, the riots… all of it feels like the same shape wearing different faces. 

The others stopped me from fighting him. He offered them an out and they took it, and dragged me along with them.

## The Vision

I had a vision in Baldwin’s borrowed castle. The room didn’t change, Baldwin kept speaking, but the space between us filled with the Nav’, the restless dead the stories say cannot find their way. These were not the Nav’ of stories. They did not glow. They seeped. A reddish-black substance, thick as oil, ran from their mouths and through the gaps in their fingers, dragging across the floor as they stumbled toward me. 

And above us, Chernobog. The Dark God, lord of death and all that cannot rest. He watched from the rafters on black wings, looking down at Baldwin standing in the middle of his harvest. 

When the vision broke, the room was empty again. I reached out and tried to feel them, but there was nothing. I tried to search the rest of the castle, but the others stopped me. They refuse to understand. The souls are crying out. They need release. 

So I took what I could. I drank Baldwin’s peach juice. It was good. A small thing, but it was mine. 

We didn’t linger. You don’t stay in a place like that unless you want to become part of it. There was talk after. Baldwin offering terms, or retreat, or both. It sounded like men trying to bargain with something they can't understand. 

The city is breaking. You can feel it in the air, like a storm already on its way. We’re leaving soon, We need to stop the end of the world.

## The Construct

We went into the castle expecting answers and instead we got a corpse. Not a person, but it felt like one. A construct laid open and hollowed out. It was missing the crystal they store souls in. They called it cooperation, said it was proof they were helping. It wasn't. Baldwin is gone, slipped out before anything real could happen, and left that thing behind to keep us busy. 

It almost worked. 

Everyone gathered around it, picking it apart, trying to understand it like if we studied it long enough it would stop being wrong. It doesn’t work like that. I knew the moment I saw it. The inside looked like veins, but not really, more like pipes pretending to be something alive, and right in the center there was space where the crystal should have been. I said it out loud before I could stop myself. A soul. No one argued, which was worse.

I had crystals from the workshop we found earlier. I kept them because I figured they would come in useful. Here we are. I tried one. Nothing happened. It fit perfectly, but the crystals I have don’t contain a soul. I jested about putting one of us in it, about donating a soul just to see if it works. It was funny at the time. It’s less funny now.

The moment we decide to use it instead of destroy it, we’re not that different from the people who built it.

We left the broken one behind. We managed to get a working one from the fool Baldwin left in charge. The machine isn’t complicated. It runs on something stolen. We won’t use it. Not now. We’ll study it when we can, but for now I’m just glad it’s out of circulation. Maybe it will make the authorities trust them a little less

## The Road

We left the city after that. The bridge, the riot, Baldwin, whatever he’s building, none of it is finished. It’s behind us now, and I have a feeling it’s also in front of us. We talked about leaving like it was the reasonable thing to do, like we’ll come back later. Maybe that’s true, but I’ve seen how later works. Later turns into never if you let it. But we do have a world to save.

There were bodies on the way out of the city. Piles of them. We didn’t do it, but we didn’t stop it either. Both are true and neither helps. We argued about where to go next and settled on heading toward the source of all of this instead of wandering around hoping to get stronger. It’s probably the right call, but it still feels like guessing.

Travel was what you’d expect. No one thinks about food until it’s a problem, so I handled it. Bought supplies, ended up with a camel. I hate that I have to spend my own gold being the responsible one.

On the road we found a fire at an oasis. For a second I thought it might be connected to everything else, but it wasn’t, just people being careless. We helped anyway. It took longer than it should have and we weren’t exactly efficient, but we got it under control. By the end of it we were covered in ash and exhausted, but it felt better than anything we did in the city.
