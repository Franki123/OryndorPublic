Chios isn’t much different. Same problems, different names. People struggling, water too expensive, food worse. I made water and handed it out. People treated it like it mattered, but it won’t unless we stop what’s coming.

There was a man at the inn with a mechanical arm and leg, the same kind of work we’ve seen before. That caught my attention immediately. Alistair says something felt off about him, and I agree, but I couldn’t prove it. No clear sign of anything wrong, no obvious source of power, just something missing. I don’t like not knowing, especially when it feels connected to everything else. We left him alone for now.

We’re moving again tomorrow toward whatever this convergence is supposed to be. No one really knows what it is, just the visions Alistair and I had. That should bother me more than it does. What bothers me is everything we’ve already seen, what we’ve already chosen, and what we’ve already left behind.
