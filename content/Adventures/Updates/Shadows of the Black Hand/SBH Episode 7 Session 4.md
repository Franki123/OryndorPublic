
**In-Game Start Date:** April 25th, 1241
**In-Game End Date:** April 26th, 1241
**Location:** [[Gravdalla]], [[The Blue Mountain]], Unknown Structure

## Through the Gates

The jizya collection at the [[Gravdalla]] gate was uneventful, a testament to the party's restraint. The guard — startled by Kunrad's armor — assessed the party's wealth and requested eight percent of declared holdings, to be paid in full for the year. Kunrad declared the party had nothing notable. The guard observed the crusader plate and proposed ten silver. Kunrad gave him one.

The church writ had some sway. The peace treaty in place between Christian and Muslim authorities meant investigators could travel freely through the city, though they could not compel cooperation. The guard accepted the writ with subdued confusion, wrote out a passport for three weeks, and handed it to the man most likely to get himself arrested.

Kunrad handed it to Wilson.

The party paid five gold apiece for individual copies. Lucero briefly considered offering the guard a vial of spirits as a goodwill gesture before three people simultaneously reminded him which territory he was in. The liquor went back in the pack.

## The Man Who Looks Like Baldwin

[[Reginald Greystone]] was already at the caravanserai when the party arrived. He had not sought them out. He was sitting alone with a drink and a journal, moving with an ease that was almost conspicuous.

[[Frigg Oden]] saw the face first. The rest of the party looked. Everyone who had met [[Baldwin Greystone]] in person noticed the resemblance immediately — the sharp northern features, the pale eyes, the jaw. He was two or three decades younger, lighter in affect, and moving through a crowded room like he owned his portion of it. It was not Baldwin.

[[Lucero De La Cruz]] approached first and offered him a drink. Reginald accepted without ceremony. By the time the party had gathered around the table, he had introduced himself: Baldwin's son, here on business for a friend, looking for someone to ascend the mountain with him. He had already spoken to the local authorities on the subject and obtained the journal of a researcher named [[Harun al-Azraq]] and had been using it as a reference.

When [[Frigg Oden]] asked directly whether he was close with his father, Reginald said no. He did not elaborate. He offered to share the journal with whoever wanted it. He recommended the party buy climbing gear.

[[Kunrad Valencius]] did not trust him. He ran **Divine Sense** and found no celestial, fiendish, or undead presence on Reginald. He continued not trusting him anyway.

The party agreed to the joint ascent.

## Gear and Departure

The market vendors knew what it meant when someone asked about the Blue Mountain. The standard kit was priced at 35 gold per person. [[Allistair Ashworth]] attempted a persuasion roll — with disadvantage, because announcing you are climbing that mountain is functionally announcing you plan to die — and got a twelve. The vendor found this charming. He sold them each a climber's kit for 30 gold, only a 5 gold mark up.

**[[William Cromwell]]** paid for the party from the 500-gold windfall he had quietly pocketed when everyone else left the room during a prior encounter. No one had asked about it until now.

Jeff and Stacy — the camels — were left at the inn. Camels do not climb mountains.

At the eastern gate, the guards warned the party it was very dangerous out that way. [[Frigg Oden]] thanked them for the information and confirmed that was what they were looking for. The guards shrugged. The party passed through.

The Blue Mountain filled the eastern sky. At its peak, a blue glow sat in the morning light — deeper than sky-blue, more deliberate. Kunrad made a religion check and confirmed it was a mountain. The party began the ascent.

## What the Mountain Shows You

Kunrad's **Divine Sense** fired about halfway up. No target within sixty feet — but something vast and celestial higher on the mountain. Far too large and too distant to pinpoint. He reported it to the group. The group continued climbing.

Above the frost line, the vegetation thinned to almost nothing. No birds. No game signs. Whatever prey would normally sustain the mountain's predators had long since stopped coming this high. A hand protruded from the packed ice at the edge of the path — a full body frozen beneath it, seated against the ice as though he had simply sat down and gone to sleep. No wounds. No bite marks. The body was frozen solid into the ground.

Kunrad attempted to extract it by the shoulder to prevent dismemberment. The hand came off in a magnificent crack. He held it for a moment and set it down.

Frigg's examination determined the man had died of cold — not cold inflicted on him, just cold. He had stopped and the mountain had done the rest.

Three routes branched upward: a path threading through jutting rock, a grove of sparse trees, and a natural archway tunnel. Reginald recommended the tunnel on grounds that they did not know what else was ahead and should save their resources. The party took the tunnel.

---

## The Combat

The trees were not empty. [[Frigg Oden]]'s attention caught two glowing points in the undergrowth — blue gems, she thought, right until the thing they were attached to launched itself at her. Combat began.

Three [[Creature - Azure Pard]]. Black fur, blue outlines, glowing eyes. Resistant to radiant damage, which Kunrad discovered by hitting one with radiant damage and watching it shrug. They were smaller than mountain lions and moved like they'd been doing nothing else for years.

Reginald demonstrated his utility immediately. He covered ground faster than anyone in the party and hit the first Pard three times with his rapier — the strikes so quick that the blood only fell after the cat did. [[Lucero De La Cruz]] moved quickly through the combat as well.

The rest of the party addressed the remaining two. Allistair hit one with **Vicious Mockery** and imposed disadvantage; Kunrad opened with a javelin hit and a **Shield of Faith**. The fight resolved cleanly.

The Pards skinned out easily. Frigg recovered a **rare Azure pelt** and a **common Azure pelt**. William's technique produced one **tattered Azure pelt**. The property that made the creatures painful to look at vanished with their deaths — in pelt form they were simply extraordinarily soft, with that faint blue outline.

Kunrad noted that the Pards had no fat. Whatever food chain normally sustained mountain predators had broken down here entirely. The Pards were hunting on instinct with nothing left to hunt.

## The Tunnel

The archway tunnel was naturally formed and wide enough for the party to walk upright. The first sign something was wrong came from Kunrad: he hit the wall with his warhammer and the sound stopped at the impact. No echo. No carry. Just the strike and then nothing, like sound went in and did not come back.

The silence was not consistent. Footsteps were sometimes audible, sometimes not. Speech crossed five feet of distance and arrived quieter than it should have. [[William Cromwell]] tapped Frigg on the shoulder to ask if she could hear him. She could — barely, and not until he was directly behind her. He could not hear her response at normal volume.

Wilson, at the back of the group, looked up. The ceiling above them was laced with cracks. They ran in parallel lines above the party's path and appeared to move — slowly, without purpose, like something shifting weight in the stone above. Wilson threw pebbles at his companions to get attention. The party communicated by proximity and gesture for the rest of the transit.

Kunrad, however, stopped moving. He was crouching at the tunnel entrance, examining the walls, occasionally hitting them, running **Divine Sense** again. The second casting registered **three ghostly undead presences** distributed around the group. He could not place them precisely. He tested whether the sediment in the walls had anything to do with the silence. It did not. He could not determine what the silence was doing or why.

Frigg and Lucero went back to retrieve him. Frigg tripped going down and took 1d6 bludgeoning on the way. Lucero helped her up. When they returned to Kunrad, Lucero slapped him back to functional attention. Kunrad explained that he thought the tunnel was dangerous and he was not sure it was wise to proceed. Lucero thought that he saw more azure pards around them, urging the group to move up the tunnel.

Kunrad refused though and the rest of the group returned back through the tunnel. [[William Cromwell]] scored a NAT 20 on his perception check and discovered 2 blue gems that [[Lucero De La Cruz]] had mistaken for the eyes of the pards. They proceeded through the section of rock teeth.

Rock teeth were warm to the touch, but had no other anomalous effects that the party noticed. They moved through the area slowly but without incident.

## What Is at the Summit

The vault-like chamber at the top of the tunnel was enormous. High ceilings. Crates stacked to one side. Bones on the floor.

Frigg examined the bones. They were not human — arm bones too long and too light, others too dense and too short, some with what appeared to be snout-shaped skulls. Multiple species. No bite marks. Different states of decay, suggesting they had accumulated over time rather than dying at once. She had never seen skeletal forms like this before.

William opened a crate. Inside, wrapped in material that felt like smooth flexible metal but was not cold to the touch, were dehydrated rations — compressed bricks of something that tasted like food but had no analogue in any supply chain the party knew. He distributed several. Frigg rolled a natural 20 on survival and identified them as the most advanced portable rations she had ever encountered. Nobody had a frame of reference to explain how they existed.

Then the construct appeared.

It was similar in concept to the mechanical guards they had encountered before — but built differently. Slighter, taller, smoother. No grinding sounds when it moved. Its head was a single large iris that changed color: **yellow** on approach (scanning), **green** when Frigg waved at it (accepting). It spoke in a flat synthetic voice: *"Follow me."*

Wilson asked what its purpose was. The eye turned **purple** briefly — processing — then answered: *"To protect."* When Frigg asked what it was protecting, the eye turned purple again and did not answer. When Allistair asked what this place was, the eye turned purple and said: *"An arc."* Then green again as it turned and continued walking.

It moved through corridors and opened doors as it approached them — sealed doors that no one had touched. It led the party through white immaculate rooms: beds unlike any the party had seen, clean geometric furniture, glass-topped desks, light sources with no visible flame. Reginald, who had been composed throughout the ascent, was visibly unsettled by the time they reached the far door.

The construct stepped aside.

The final room had rows of glass desks, scattered chairs, and at the center: a raised seat with controls on both armrests, positioned above the others. At the far end of the room, a window — completely dark. As the party entered, the window lit up.

A woman appeared in the frame. She was not quite right: too tall, too slight, proportions that didn't resolve correctly at first glance. Her voice came from everywhere at once — a melodic, unhurried tone that felt like it had been specifically calibrated to be safe. She said:

*"Hello, travelers. My name is Mother."*

## Party Status

The party is inside the structure on top of the mountain, the air is cruel and thin. The inside is warm and comforting, but the party is exhausted and would be at a disadvantage should Mother prove... Troublesome.
