title: The Second Crusade
---
**Duration:** 1173 – 1240

**Outcome:** Expansion and consolidation of [[Christian]] holdings in the eastern provinces; widespread atrocities; collapse of crusader morale and institutional credibility

The Second Crusade began roughly a generation after the First's formal conclusion. Where the [[First Crusade]] had ultimately been about retaking and protecting holy cities, the Second was about holding and extending it: pushing east into provinces where Muslim resistance had never fully broken, suppressing resurgent populations in cities already occupied, and consolidating Christian governance over territories that remained hostile beneath the surface.

It lasted 67 years and ended in 1240. It did not end cleanly.

---

## Background

### The Problem of Occupation

The [[First Crusade]] had won cities. The Second inherited the question no recruitment sermon had answered: what do you do with a million people who do not want to be governed by you?

The [[Christian]] occupation had always rested on a fragile equilibrium. The [[Muslim]] majorities in cities like [[Lektas]] were not subdued — they were present, patient, and waiting. Trade continued. Taxes were collected. The mosques remained open. The [[Christian]] lords, dependent on local administrative knowledge and commercial activity, found it easier to tolerate the existing social fabric than to dismantle it. For generations, an uneasy stability held.

By 1165, that stability was fraying. Renewed [[Muslim]] political consolidation to the east created a rallying point for resistance movements within the occupied territories. Insurgent networks operated through trade guilds, religious institutions, and family ties stretching across borders. The nobility of [[Europe]] read these developments as the [[First Crusade]]'s unfinished business and prompted the Church to proclaim a new campaign — framed as defense of what had been won rather than conquest of what hadn't.

### Recruitment and Expansion

The Second Crusade's call drew a different composition than the First. The great sweep of noble houses and their armies had already come and gone; the [[Iron Covenant]]'s founding lords were now settled landowners with no interest in further disruption. What the Second Crusade raised was a mix of second-generation true believers, younger sons of crusader-settler families who had grown up in the occupied territories, and a significant influx of clergy and support personnel who believed they were doing humanitarian work.

The [[Order of the Radiant Cross]] drove recruitment aggressively, particularly for roles the order had chronically underfilled: field medics, chaplains, and administrative clergy. The sermons promised that healers would save lives, not end them. The honest version of that promise depended on who was doing the fighting.

## The Orders

The Second Crusade saw the emergence of new specialized orders designed to handle the complexities of long-term occupation and insurgency, while the legacy orders of the [[First Crusade]] adapted to their new, often more cynical, roles.

### New Orders of the Second Crusade

- **[[Order of the Vigilant Sun]]:** Formed specifically to counter internal resistance, this order functioned as the Crusade’s intelligence and internal security arm. They specialized in identifying insurgent networks within occupied cities, often employing the most controversial  methods of the Suppression Campaigns.
- **[[Order of God's Gate]]:** A heavy infantry and garrison order tasked with the physical management of occupied urban centers and the defense of the expanding frontier. They were the "standing wall" of the Crusade, focused on riot control and maintaining a constant, intimidating military presence.
- **[[Gilded Arbiters]]:** A secular-ecclesiastical hybrid focused on the logistics and economics of the Crusade. They managed the taxation of occupied populations and the extraction of resources from the eastern provinces, ensuring the campaign remained self-sustaining even as its moral authority waned.

### Roles of the Legacy Orders

- **[[Order of the Radiant Cross]]:** The primary driver and strategic backbone of the Second Crusade. They transitioned from a conquering force to an aggressive offensive and recruitment body, leading the Eastern Push while systematically hollowing out other orders to fill their specialized personnel gaps.
- **[[Knights of the Iron Covenant]]:** Now the established nobility of the [[Principality of Murionia]], the Covenant took a largely defensive and skeptical stance. They provided mandatory levies but resisted further expansion that threatened the stability of their own settled territories.
- **[[Brotherhood of the Penitent Blade]]:** Reduced to a fanatic remnant, they served as the Crusade’s "forlorn hope" in the East. They were deployed in the most exposed positions and used for high-casualty "cleansing" operations where survival was not expected.
- **[[Hospitallers of St. Andrian]]:** Struggled to maintain their healing mandate amidst escalating atrocities. Poached for medics by the Radiant Cross and restricted by military commanders, many Hospitallers eventually broke their vows to form the foundation of the [[Brotherhood of the Broken Oath]].

## Campaigns

### The Eastern Push (1153 – ~1180)

The early decades of the Second Crusade were the most conventional. Christian forces pushed east into provinces that had never been fully incorporated into the First Crusade's territorial gains, capturing fortified towns and establishing garrison lines. The [[Order of the Radiant Cross]] led most of the offensive operations, supplemented by levies from the settled crusader nobility.

The campaigns were successful by the narrow measure of territory taken and held. They were contested by the broader measure of what holding territory required. Captured towns found their populations uncooperative, their supply chains subject to constant disruption, and their garrison troops spread thin across an expanding frontier.

### The Suppression Campaigns (~1180 – ~1220)

The middle decades shifted from external conquest to internal suppression. Resistance movements within the occupied territories grew more organized and more willing to employ violence against both military and civilian targets. The crusading orders responded with escalating force.

This is the period that would define the Second Crusade's legacy. Field commanders, operating with broad mandate and minimal oversight, made decisions that crossed lines the Church hierarchy either did not know about or preferred not to acknowledge. The practices that had begun as tactical necessities — collective punishment, preemptive strikes against suspected insurgent networks, the classification of entire villages as legitimate military targets — became routine.

#### Phais

[[Phais]] was a village in the eastern provinces flagged by military intelligence as harboring insurgent sympathizers. The intelligence was disputed; some commanders believed the population was coerced rather than complicit. The distinction was not observed.

Soldiers dragged the village's population into the central square and promptly "cleansed." It was later found that the villagers were blameless. The enemy had come into the town and forced the villagers to give up supplies and their children to the cause.  

The Church would later demand an investigation into the matter, the [[Order of the Radiant Cross]] the order in charge of the operation, preformed an investigation but found all the participants too valuable to give up to punishment and so several lower level officers were punished instead

[[Phais]] became an example of the brutality of the crusades, one of several, but certainly the most cited example.

### The Final Years (~1220 – 1240)

The last two decades of the Second Crusade saw both a reduction in active military campaigning and a hardening of occupation policies in controlled territories. The eastern push had reached its practical limit; the suppression campaigns had produced diminishing returns and a growing recruitment problem, as reports from returning veterans circulated through Christian communities at home.

The crusade formally ended in 1240, concluded by treaty rather than victory — the eastern expansion abandoned, the existing occupation codified, the question of what had been done set aside rather than answered.

## Aftermath

### Order of the Vigilant Sun
After the crusades ended the Vigilant sun was supposedly dispersed. However, those that were privy to such matters knew that the order had gone underground. Nobody outside the order knows to what end.

### Order of God's Gate
Still in operation, they act as a sort of mobile army that nobles in the conquered territories sometimes call on. Being primarily defensive, they did not have the opportunity to earn the fame/infamy that the [[Order of the Radiant Cross]] did. 

They are a fighting force of average effectiveness.

### Gilded Arbiters
The arbiters abandoned military conflict in order to pursue a monetary conflict and formed a collection of the foremost known banks in Christian Africa. From this position they provide aid and organize logistics of the kingdoms that now rule the area.

### Brotherhood of the Penitent Blade
Having committed their own (smaller scale but many times worse) atrocities during the crusade the penitent blade found itself at the end of the crusade with few members and large allegations. The order was disbanded and any remaining members are living out their lives in disgrace.

### Hospitallers of St. Andrian

Between members that were lost (either to combat or poaching from the Radiant Cross) and the need for the remaining members to distance from the crusades, the Hospitallers was disbanded after the crusade. The remaining members formed the [[Brotherhood of the Broken Oath]]. Which serves as a loose fellowship of former crusaders who have renounced their vows after witnessing the campaign's practices. They are not rebels or reformers in any organized sense. They are people who have broken under the weight of what they have seen or done, and found that others have broken in the same places.

The Radiant Cross considers all members of the [[Brotherhood of the Broken Oath]] cowards.

### The Schism of the Radiant Cross

The Second Crusade's conduct accelerated the internal fracture already developing within the [[Order of the Radiant Cross]]. The Veterans, those who fought in the campaigns and defend what was done, face increasing pressure from the Reformers, largely composed of clergy who arrived later and were appalled by what they found.

### The Occupation

Ultimately, the second crusade accomplished little in the way of expansion or consolidation. What was gained in territory was mostly lost by the end and what was gained in order was lost in good will. 

Christians familiar with the Second Crusades regard it as the ultimate in service to God or as a stain on the Churches reputation in the Muslim world.

[[Muslims]], especially those being ruled over by [[Christians]], regard both the [[First Crusade]] and the Second less as atrocities and more as obstacles to their own great empire and care little for the Christians' attempts to atone themselves in their eyes.
