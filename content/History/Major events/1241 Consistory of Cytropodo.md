**Location:** [[Cytropodo]], the Papal Court
**Date:** Spring, 1241 AD

---

The hall is cool even in spring. Forty men in red sit at a curved table of dark wood — cardinals, their faces studies in careful neutrality. At the center of the room stands something that does not belong: a [[Mechanical Man]], inert, its joints locked, eye-lenses dark. It has been chained to a wheeled platform. Three servants in the livery of Cardinal [[Matteo Aldovari]] stand at respectful distance nearby, uncertain whether to treat it as prisoner or exhibit.

Cardinal Aldovari stands before the table. He is a broad, florid man with the voice of someone accustomed to filling cathedrals. He presents the Mechanical Soldier to the council as an *instrument of peacekeeping* — a tool that eliminates the need to risk Christian lives in the enforcement of God's order. He is emphatic, compelling, perhaps a touch too eager. He produces letters of endorsement, casualty reports from field tests, and an accounting of riots suppressed. Several cardinals lean in. A few ask pointed questions about the source of the power animating the machine. Aldovari deflects smoothly — *proprietary mechanisms, sealed research under review*.

The room is moving toward consensus. Then the Pope speaks.

> *"Cardinal Aldovari. You have shown us a remarkable thing. You have not shown us what manner of soul was required to make it move."*

Aldovari begins to answer. The Pope raises one hand.

> *"The Church does not arm itself with instruments whose nature it does not understand. Whatever animates this construct — whether mechanism, spirit, or something worse — I will not have it deployed in His name until we know. Disassemble it. Have the parts inventoried and sealed. We will review the findings when they are complete."*

A beat of silence. Then [[Bishop Erasmus Thorn]] speaks from the far end of the table. His voice is quiet. Everyone hears it.

> *"Your Holiness. If I may. The disassembly of such a construct is a delicate matter requiring precision and mechanical expertise that most of our scholars do not possess. I would be honored to head the research — to ensure the process is conducted with the rigor the Church demands. I would report my findings to this council personally."*

Several cardinals nod. It is, on its face, a reasonable offer. Thorn continues without changing his tone.

> *"In the meantime, the construct poses no threat while under Church custody. Its continued operation — in a controlled capacity — would allow the research to be conducted on a functioning subject rather than scattered components. The findings would be far more complete."*

The Pope considers. The room waits. He looks at Thorn for a long moment — not suspicious, simply thinking.

> *"No, Bishop Thorn. I appreciate the offer. Disassemble it."*

He does not explain further. He does not need to.

---

## The Corridor

Several minutes later. The council has dispersed. The marble corridor outside is long, lit by high narrow windows.

Cardinal Aldovari catches up to [[Bishop Erasmus Thorn]] in the corridor. His footsteps are quick, slightly desperate. Thorn does not slow down.

**Aldovari:** *"Erasmus — Erasmus, wait. I know this is not the outcome we hoped for. But the Pope is old. He is cautious. If we bring more documentation — more testimony from [[Lektas]], perhaps from a respected commander—"*

Thorn does not respond. He walks.

**Aldovari:** *"I was persuasive in there. You saw it. The room was with me before he spoke. I simply need another opportunity. If you could speak to the right people, arrange for a smaller audience, something more private — I will do better. I promise you. Just give me another chance."*

At the end of the corridor there is a heavy door. Thorn opens it, steps through, and closes it behind him.

Aldovari stands alone in the hall.
