title: The First Crusade
---
**Duration:** 1032 – 1152

**Outcome:** Christian occupation of most of Africa, including [[Lektas]]

The First Crusade was a prolonged series of military campaigns waged by the Christian powers of the [[Old World]] against the various Muslim held territories, mostly across southern Europe and northern Africa. Proclaimed in 1032 and lasting 120 years across multiple distinct campaigns, it was not a single war but a succession of advances, reverses, consolidations, and renewed offensives bound together under the same religious mandate. Its conclusion left a patchwork of Christian-occupied cities and kingdoms presiding over Muslim majorities who had never consented to be governed by them.

The [[Second Crusade]] followed roughly a generation later, inheriting the First's unresolved tensions.

---

## Background

### The Call to Holy War

The crusade was proclaimed by the Council of [[Stymphid]] under Pope [[Pope Celestine IV]] in response to the advance of Muslim powers into territories it considered sacred or strategically vital to Christendom. Papal legates traveled across the Christian kingdoms preaching the same essential sermon: the faithful were being slaughtered, holy sites desecrated, and the darkness would reach the homeland if left unchecked. The promise extended to all who took the cross — remission of sins, protection of estates during absence, and a place in paradise for those who fell in the holy cause.

The reality of who answered the call was complicated. True believers came in great numbers. So did landless younger sons, debtors seeking to outrun their creditors, mercenaries who found the crusading indulgence a convenient absolution, and lords who saw opportunity in the distribution of conquered land. The crusade was a holy war, but the motives of all involved were not entirely pure.

## The Orders

The crusade was prosecuted by a coalition of independent military orders operating under loose ecclesiastical coordination. They shared the broad goal of conquest and conversion but differed sharply in method, theology, and ambition. Their rivalries were nearly as significant as their cooperation.

### Order of the Radiant Cross

The most powerful and most infamous of the crusading orders. The [[Order of the Radiant Cross]] was founded as the Church's primary militant arm and purpose-built for the first crusade, giving it a foundational claim to authority that older orders resented. Its knights operated under direct papal sanction, which gave field commanders broad latitude to act without consulting secular lords or local Church authorities.

In the early campaigns, the Radiant Cross provided the crusade's strategic backbone — its forces were disciplined, its logistics relatively sound, and its commanders willing to prosecute sieges through the bitter years-long waits that took other orders' patience apart. They were efficient, strategic and forward thinking.

However, as the campaigns progressed, the commanders that would have shown mercy were punished for it and killed by the enemy they fought and the Radiant was drained of all its mercy via attrition and by the end of the campaign was known as the most brutal order of them all, having committed countless atrocities. 

*See: [[Order of the Radiant Cross]]*

### Knights of the Iron Covenant

A secular-military order formed not by the Church but by a compact among six noble houses who pledged mutual obligation to the crusade. Where the Radiant Cross answered to the papacy, the Iron Covenant answered to its founding lords — which meant it was simultaneously more politically flexible and more susceptible to internal faction.

The Covenant's strength was administrative. Their lords had experience governing conquered territory, managing tax collection, and negotiating with subject populations rather than simply subduing them. In the cities they held, the transition to Christian rule tended to be slower and less violent than under Radiant Cross governance. Their weakness was that any campaign requiring unified action among the founding lords required negotiation among men with competing interests. At critical moments, the Covenant moved slowly.

The order gradually transitioned from a military force to a governing aristocracy as the crusade's conquests were consolidated. By the end of the First Crusade, the Iron Covenant transitioned into it's own kingdom - the [[Principality of Murionia]] 

### Brotherhood of the Penitent Blade

A monastic military order whose members took vows of poverty, obedience, and what their founders called "holy suffering in the name of the Cross." The Brotherhood believed that the highest form of crusading was to seek out the most dangerous work — the advance scouts, the forlorn hopes, the garrison troops in the most exposed positions — and accept death as a form of prayer. They did not fight to win renown. They fought to die well.

This theology made them extraordinarily useful in the early phases of the crusade, when someone had to hold a position against impossible odds while the main force organized a relief. It also made them erratic allies in longer campaigns, where the Brotherhood's appetite for sacrifice occasionally read as tactical recklessness. They were never large in number but maintained an outsized presence in the crusade's mythology — partly because dead men accumulate legend faster than survivors.

Their extreme piety put them in recurring tension with the Radiant Cross, whose more pragmatic approach to holy war the Brotherhood considered insufficiently holy. The rivalry occasionally turned violent.

### The Hospitallers of St. Andrian

Founded as a healing order attached to a pilgrimage hospital predating the crusade, the [[Hospitallers of St. Andrian]] were drafted into the military effort by necessity and transformed by it. Their original mandate was care for the sick and dying, regardless of faith. The crusade did not change that mandate officially. It simply placed the Hospitallers in a context where following it required a particular kind of courage.

In practice, Andrian healers moved through every phase of the crusade — treating crusaders, and when circumstances permitted, the wounded on the other side. They established field hospitals, managed supply lines, and negotiated prisoner exchanges. Over the 120 years of the First Crusade, they became indispensable infrastructure for the Christian host.

They also accumulated resentments. Military commanders found the Hospitallers' insistence on treating enemy wounded — and their documented refusal to participate in certain operations — a persistent irritant. The [[Radiant Cross and the Brotherhood]] of the [[Penitent Blade]] both considered the Andrians insufficiently zealous. The Hospitallers developed a reputation for quiet, stubborn insubordination in service of their original oath.

Their legacy fed directly into the tensions of the [[Second Crusade]], when the Order of the Radiant Cross began aggressively recruiting field medics to fill gaps in the Andrians' coverage.

The remained largely blameless of the atrocities committed by the other orders.

## Campaigns

The First Crusade unfolded across three broad phases separated by periods of consolidation, political crisis among the crusading powers, and renewed Muslim resistance.

**The First Wave** last approximately 10 years. The enthusiastic Christian force swept across the Muslim lands faster than the opposing force could properly unite. Lordships were established to maintain order, but the Christians found that they had taken too much territory to govern effectively.

**The Long Middle** lasted roughly 80 years of the crusade's 120-year span and was characterized by grinding attritional conflict. Territory changed hands repeatedly. Alliances between Muslim political entities formed as local interests outweighed ideological ones. The crusading orders competed as much with each other for influence and land grants as with the Muslim defenders.

**The Final Campaigns** represented the last 30 years of the conflict and was prosecuted with deliberate, systematic and brutal effort rather than the first wave's momentum. The orders, now experienced  in the region's terrain and cynical of its inhabitants, worked more methodically to consolidate and hold what they took. The orders committed most of their atrocities during these campaigns.

## Outcome and Legacy

The First Crusade ended with a patchwork of Christian-governed cities and territories spread around the target areas. The crusaders had won occupation without achieving conversion or assimilation. The populations they ruled remained predominantly [[Muslim]], governed by a [[Christian]] minority that held the citadels and the administrative apparatus but never transformed the culture beneath them.

The military orders that prosecuted the First Crusade evolved accordingly:

- The [[Order of the Radiant Cross]] transitioned from a conquering force to a peacekeeping and enforcement body. 
- The [[Iron Covenant]] became the nobility of the occupied territories. 
- The [[Brotherhood of the Penitent Blade]] shrank to a remnant of true believers. 
- The [[Hospitallers of St. Andrian]] continued their work until the [[Second Crusade]]'s recruitment drives hollowed out their ranks.
